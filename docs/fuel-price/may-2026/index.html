What moved it

Each month's change is set by the review period before it, here from Friday 27 March 2026 to Wednesday 29 April 2026. Every day of it, CEF compares what fuel actually cost to import (the Basic Fuel Price) with the cost already built into the pump price. The period's average gap, the over- or under-recovery, is how much the price has to move to catch up.

Petrol 95 cost on average R2.04 a litre more to import than was built into the price (an under-recovery). Its import cost averaged R15.78 a litre. On its own that called for a change of +R2.04. The official change was +R3.27, R1.23 more: the rest comes from decisions outside the Basic Fuel Price, such as slate-levy and fuel-levy changes, which the government's announcement sets out.

Diesel (0.05% sulphur) cost on average R4.96 a litre more to import than was built into the price (an under-recovery). Its import cost averaged R23.67 a litre. On its own that called for a change of +R4.96. The official change was +R5.27, 30c more: the rest comes from decisions outside the Basic Fuel Price, such as slate-levy and fuel-levy changes, which the government's announcement sets out.

The rand averaged R16.65 to the US dollar over the period, about the same as the period before (R16.64).

Figures from CEF's daily Basic Fuel Price reports (cefgroup.co.za): prices from its pump, wholesale and maximum retail price rows, the averages from the last report of the period.
